**Brand focus:** K-beauty aesthetics clinic for women across Greater Vancouver — not spa, not physiotherapy

## 1. Brand Core

### Brand Essence

> **Medical confidence with Korean refinement.**

| Element | Direction |
|---|---|
| Category | K-beauty aesthetics clinic — medical aesthetics, not spa or physiotherapy |
| Audience | Women across Greater Vancouver who appreciate Korean aesthetics and culture |
| Difference | South Korean cosmetic experience, expertise with Asian skin and extensive treatment history |

### Positioning Statement

K Beauty Clinic is a K-beauty aesthetics clinic serving women across Greater Vancouver who appreciate Korean beauty and culture. Led by extensive South Korean cosmetic experience, the clinic combines advanced treatments, medical credibility and personalized care to deliver confident, visible results.

### Strategic Exclusion

The brand is an aesthetics clinic. It is not a spa, physiotherapy clinic, wellness studio, naturopathic practice or IV lounge. Communication must remain focused on injectables, advanced lasers, medical skincare and facial aesthetic treatments.

## 7. Voice and Writing

The voice should be **professional, educational, reassuring and results-focused**.

| Voice Trait | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Professional | Specific language, calm confidence and correct treatment terminology | Casual slang or over-familiarity |
| Educational | Explain what, why, suitability, process and recovery | Information overload or unexplained jargon |
| Reassuring | Realistic expectations, safety and thoughtful guidance | Fear-based selling |
| Results-focused | Evidence, progress and client goals | Guaranteed, perfect or permanent claims |

### Writing Principles

- Lead with the client concern, then explain the professional solution.
- Use plain English first; add clinical terms only when they improve understanding.
- Describe Korean expertise through experience, methods and standards—not stereotypes.
- Write inclusively for Korean, Chinese, other Asian and Caucasian women.
- Always qualify results and avoid medical or cosmetic guarantees.

### Preferred Language

- Personalized plan
- Professional assessment
- Advanced Korean aesthetics
- Visible improvement
- Realistic results
- Confidence

### Words to Avoid

- Flawless
- Perfect
- Miracle
- Instant transformation
- Guaranteed
- Cheap
- Beauty defect

### Approved Slogans

Public-facing slogans are confined to these three ranked lines. Do not invent additional taglines for ads, signage or the website. They are not medical claims and must not be presented as a guarantee of outcome.

| Rank | Slogan | Best use |

| 2 | Medical Confidence with Korean Refinement. | Homepage headline and premium campaigns |

## 10. Colour Strategy

The original three palettes avoid strong red and blue. Each uses a warm-neutral foundation so photography and real skin tones remain the visual focus. Three further explorations follow the same rules for comparison.

### Palette Comparison

| Palette | Strategic Character | Best Use |
|---|---|---|
| **01 Hanji Forest—Recommended** | Strongest balance of Korean refinement, medical trust and warmth | Master brand, website, signage and clinical materials |
| **02 Seoul Stone** | Neutral, modern and architectural | Premium editorial campaigns and minimalist interiors |
| **03 Champagne Porcelain** | Softer, more feminine and beauty-forward | Social campaigns, client materials and premium promotions |
| **04 Scholar Ink—Exploration** | Calligraphic authority: ink, paper and editorial restraint | Credentials, practitioner story and long-form education |
| **05 Celadon Mist—Exploration** | Ceramic calm: mineral grey-green without hospital blue or spa mint | Clinical materials and treatment explanation pages |
| **06 Persimmon Silk—Exploration** | Mature warmth from dried persimmon and silk, not strong red | Beauty-forward campaigns that still feel medical |

### Colour Usage Principle

- 60% light neutral
- 25% dark anchor
- 10% supporting neutral
- 5% accent

Accent colour should guide attention rather than decorate every element.

## 11. Colour System 01—Hanji Forest

**Status:** Recommended

Deep Forest establishes medical trust without relying on conventional blue. Hanji Ivory and Sand Taupe bring warmth and cultural refinement; Soft Gold adds premium detail without looking flashy.

### Brand reasoning

The brand essence is **medical confidence with Korean refinement**. Deep Forest carries confidence and clinical responsibility without the hospital blue the guideline forbids. Hanji Ivory is a paper-like 60% ground so real skin and before-and-after photography stay dominant. Soft Gold is the 5% accent: premium and accessible, never gold-gradient luxury.

- **Personality:** Clinical with warmth (2/5), premium but welcoming (4/5).
- **Values:** Safety and professionalism in the dark green; Korean expertise in the paper and gold materials, not in costume or flags.
- **Watch-out:** Do not push the green toward mint or spa wellness, which the brand excludes.

| Colour | HEX | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Forest | `#23463F` | Primary anchor |
| Clinical Ink | `#172522` | Text and high contrast |
| Hanji Ivory | `#F7F2E8` | Main background |
| Sand Taupe | `#CBBBA8` | Supporting neutral |
| Soft Gold | `#B08B57` | Premium accent |

### Usage

| Purpose | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Primary background | Hanji Ivory—approximately 60% |
| Primary anchor | Deep Forest—approximately 25% |
| Supporting colour | Sand Taupe—approximately 10% |
| Accent | Soft Gold—approximately 5% |
| Accessibility | Deep Forest on Hanji Ivory has approximately 9.3:1 contrast and passes WCAG AA for normal text. |

## Colour System 06—Persimmon Silk

**Status:** Additional exploration

### Brand reasoning

Clients should feel **safe, confident and informed**. Persimmon Silk is the warmer campaign sister to Champagne Porcelain: dried persimmon is a Korean material colour that sits next to skin without becoming a strong red. Silk greige keeps photography dominant; Silk Umber keeps the system medical rather than cute.

- Combine every palette colour at equal strength.
- Introduce strong red or blue without revising the guideline.

## 14. Typography

The typography system should combine premium editorial character with clinical readability and support English and Korean.

| Role | English Recommendation | Korean Recommendation | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display and headlines | DM Serif Display | Noto Serif KR | Major headlines, campaign statements and selected service titles |
| Body and interface | Inter | Noto Sans KR | Website, forms, descriptions, navigation and captions |
| System fallback | Arial or Helvetica | Noto Sans KR | Office documents and platforms without brand fonts |

### Hierarchy

| Level | Style | Guideline |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Display serif, 38–52 px | Short, confident and spacious |
| H2 | Display serif or bold sans, 26–34 px | Major sections |
| H3 | Sans serif semibold, 18–22 px | Services and cards |
| Body | Sans serif regular, 16–18 px | Minimum 16 px for website reading |
| Caption | Sans serif medium, 13–14 px | Use sparingly and maintain contrast |

Use serif typography for premium Korean-editorial character and sans serif typography for medical clarity. Never use script fonts for body copy or treatment information.

## 15. Photography and Graphic Language

### Photography Direction

| Area | Direction |
|---|---|
| People | Feature Korean, Chinese, other Asian and Caucasian women; prioritize the 35–60+ audience while maintaining age diversity |
| Skin | Retain natural texture; use light retouching and avoid impossible perfection |
| Clinic | Use real practitioners, real rooms and real equipment whenever possible |
| Before-and-after | Standardize angle, expression, lighting, crop and timing; include consent and results-vary language |
| Mood | Clean, bright and refined with soft directional light |
| Composition | Calm close portraits, uncluttered backgrounds and purposeful negative space |

### Graphic Elements

- Use a restrained signature-line motif to connect sections or highlight treatment journeys.
- Use minimal icons with consistent line weight.
- Use clean diagrams when they improve treatment understanding.
- Avoid cute graphics, generic stock imagery, stereotypes and short-lived trends.

### Before-and-After Priority

Before-and-after evidence is a primary brand asset. Accuracy, consent, consistent conditions and realistic presentation are mandatory.
