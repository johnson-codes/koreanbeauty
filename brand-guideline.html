Brand Guideline

Strategic, verbal and visual direction for K Beauty Clinic, a K-beauty aesthetics clinic. Use the approved Korean Beauty Clinic lockup as the official logo. Do not use the retired KSA lockup.

Positioning Statement

K Beauty Clinic is a K-beauty aesthetics clinic serving women across Greater Vancouver who appreciate Korean beauty and culture. Led by extensive South Korean cosmetic experience, the clinic combines advanced treatments, medical credibility and personalized care to deliver confident, visible results.

Strategic Exclusion

The brand is an aesthetics clinic. It is not a spa, physiotherapy clinic, wellness studio, naturopathic practice or IV lounge. Communication must remain focused on injectables, advanced lasers, medical skincare and facial aesthetic treatments.

7. Voice and Writing

The voice should be professional, educational, reassuring and results-focused.

Voice TraitUseAvoid
ProfessionalSpecific language, calm confidence and correct treatment terminologyCasual slang or over-familiarity
EducationalExplain what, why, suitability, process and recoveryInformation overload or unexplained jargon
ReassuringRealistic expectations, safety and thoughtful guidanceFear-based selling
Results-focusedEvidence, progress and client goalsGuaranteed, perfect or permanent claims

Writing Principles

  • Lead with the client concern, then explain the professional solution.
  • Use plain English first; add clinical terms only when they improve understanding.
  • Describe Korean expertise through experience, methods and standards—not stereotypes.
  • Write inclusively for Korean, Chinese, other Asian and Caucasian women.
  • Always qualify results and avoid medical or cosmetic guarantees.

Preferred Language

  • Personalized plan
  • Professional assessment
  • Advanced Korean aesthetics
  • Visible improvement
  • Realistic results
  • Confidence

Words to Avoid

  • Flawless
  • Perfect
  • Miracle
  • Instant transformation
  • Guaranteed
  • Cheap
  • Beauty defect

Approved Slogans

Public-facing slogans are confined to these three ranked lines. Do not invent additional taglines for ads, signage or the website. They are not medical claims and must not be presented as a guarantee of outcome.

The original three palettes avoid strong red and blue. Each uses a warm-neutral foundation so photography and real skin tones remain the visual focus. Three further explorations follow the same rules for comparison.

Colour Usage Principle

  • 60% light neutral
  • 25% dark anchor
  • 10% supporting neutral
  • 5% accent

Accent colour should guide attention rather than decorate every element.

11. Colour System 01 — Hanji Forest

Status: Recommended master. This site uses Hanji Forest as the working brand palette.

Deep Forest establishes medical trust without relying on conventional blue. Hanji Ivory and Sand Taupe bring warmth and cultural refinement; Soft Gold adds premium detail without looking flashy.

Brand reasoning

The brand essence is medical confidence with Korean refinement. Deep Forest carries confidence and clinical responsibility without the hospital blue the guideline forbids. Hanji Ivory is a paper-like 60% ground so real skin and before-and-after photography stay dominant. Soft Gold is the 5% accent: premium and accessible, never gold-gradient luxury.

  • Personality: Clinical with warmth (2/5), premium but welcoming (4/5).
  • Values: Safety and professionalism in the dark green; Korean expertise in the paper/gold materials, not in costume or flags.
  • Watch-out: Do not push the green toward mint or spa wellness, which the brand excludes.

12. Colour System 02 — Seoul Stone

Status: Modern alternative

A quiet architectural palette inspired by contemporary Seoul interiors. It feels highly modern and sophisticated, with less cultural symbolism and more international premium appeal.

Brand reasoning

The personality spectrum is traditional → modern 4/5: contemporary and forward-looking. Seoul Stone lets Korean expertise read as lived clinical experience, not as themed decoration. Graphite is credible; Warm White keeps the 60% light field; Soft Sage is clinical support without blue; Cocoa is a warm accent that is not flashy gold.

  • Audience: Inclusive for Korean, Chinese, other Asian and Caucasian clients who appreciate Korean culture without being shown palace, flag, or stereotype colour.
  • Competitive contrast: More architectural than a salon; more restrained than a generic medical spa.
  • Watch-out: If Graphite and Sage dominate, it can feel cold. Keep Warm White at approximately 60%.

13. Colour System 03 — Champagne Porcelain

Status: Softer alternative

A softer and more feminine system that still feels mature. Porcelain and greige preserve cleanliness, while Espresso and Champagne create premium contrast for female-focused campaigns.

Brand reasoning

The primary audience is women approximately 35–60+. Personality is feminine → gender-neutral 3/5: refined femininity without stereotypes. Porcelain keeps medical cleanliness; Espresso keeps authority; Champagne is a 5% premium cue, not body text and not cute pink.

  • Market level: Premium but accessible—never intimidatingly luxurious.
  • Applications: Social, client materials and promotions, where the content mix allows about 10% offers without looking discount-led.
  • Watch-out: Overusing Champagne can drift toward salon beauty. Keep Espresso for structure and Porcelain for space.

Colour System 04 — Scholar Ink

Status: Additional exploration

Brand reasoning

Korean expertise must be a meaningful advantage, not a decorative theme. Scholar Ink uses calligraphy materials—ink and paper—so authority feels editorial and clinical rather than costumed. Dried persimmon is the accent instead of traditional seal-red, which would break the “no strong red” rule.

  • Voice: Professional, educational and reassuring. Best for credentials, practitioner story (about 20% of content) and treatment education (about 35%).
  • Difference from Hanji Forest: Removes green and gold so Korean-ness is literary and precise, not botanical.
  • Watch-out: Too much Ink can feel severe. Keep Paper Ivory at approximately 60% so the clinic still feels warm enough for comfort.

Colour System 05 — Celadon Mist

Status: Additional exploration

Brand reasoning

Professionalism should feel prepared, precise and credible. Goryeo celadon is Korean refinement through ceramic craft: mineral grey-green, not forest branding, not spa mint, and not hospital blue. Oxidized bronze is the 5% accent so labels never sit on a mid-tone green.

  • Personality: Clinical authority with enough warmth for comfort (2/5). Fits treatment explanation, equipment and protocol proof points.
  • Strategic exclusion: Stays away from wellness, IV and naturopathic cues that mint-green often signals.
  • Watch-out: Do not pair celadon with temple, lotus or palace graphics. Keep the graphic language minimal and medical.

Colour Rules

Do: use generous light space and dark, readable typography; keep treatment photography visually dominant; use accents selectively for calls to action.

Do not: use accent colours for long body text; combine every palette colour at equal strength; introduce strong red or blue without revising the guideline.

14. Typography

The typography system should combine premium editorial character with clinical readability and support English and Korean.

RoleEnglishKoreanUse
Display and headlinesDM Serif DisplayNoto Serif KRMajor headlines, campaign statements and selected service titles
Body and interfaceInterNoto Sans KRWebsite, forms, descriptions, navigation and captions
System fallbackArial or HelveticaNoto Sans KROffice documents and platforms without brand fonts

Use serif typography for premium Korean-editorial character and sans serif typography for medical clarity. Never use script fonts for body copy or treatment information.

15. Photography and Graphic Language

AreaDirection
PeopleFeature Korean, Chinese, other Asian and Caucasian women; prioritize the 35–60+ audience while maintaining age diversity
SkinRetain natural texture; use light retouching and avoid impossible perfection
ClinicUse real practitioners, real rooms and real equipment whenever possible

Graphic Elements

  • Use a restrained signature-line motif to connect sections or highlight treatment journeys.
  • Use bold, large icons (weight 700, 32px) so treatments and CTAs scan quickly. Do not use hairline icons.
  • Use clean diagrams when they improve treatment understanding.
  • Avoid cute graphics, generic stock imagery, stereotypes and short-lived trends.

Before-and-after evidence is a primary brand asset. Accuracy, consent, consistent conditions and realistic presentation are mandatory.
